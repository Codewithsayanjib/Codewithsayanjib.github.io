✓ Accepted COMSYS 2026
Evaluating SLMs on Understanding Structured Frameworks using Cluster Aware Prompting
Sur, S., and Bhaumik, P.
7th International Conference on Frontiers in Computing and Systems (COMSYS 2026)
▾
Designed a cluster-aware prompting pipeline partitioning 5 SLMs (1.5B–7B) into context-length groups (2K–4K, 8K, 16K+) with distinct zero-shot, one-shot, and self-augmented prompt families. Conducted 10,800 inference runs per model across 6 task types, 6 serialization formats (HTML, JSON, Markdown, Turtle, TXT, XML), and 5 questionnaire datasets using Ollama on Google Colab Pro (A100). Best overall accuracy: Vicuna-7B (35%); multi-hop inference collapsed below 6% across all models, exposing a hard reasoning ceiling in current SLMs.

04
⏳ Under Review IEEE CALCON 2026
NumFmt-SLM: Are Small Language Models Invariant to Number Surface Formats?
Sur, S., Sil Sarma, A., and Singh, P. K.
Submitted to IEEE Calcutta Conference (CALCON 2026)
▾
A reproducible benchmark testing whether mathematically equivalent number formats (plain, comma, words, scientific, abbreviated) change model predictions — evaluating 9 SLMs (0.6B–7B) across 7 datasets including AQuA-RAT, MMLU-Quant, GSM8K, and FinanceBench. Introduced Format Sensitivity Score (FSS) and Format Flip Rate (FFR) metrics; found 18–86% of MCQ answers flip under format changes alone. Format changes destroy correct answers ~1.7× more often than they create them.
⌥ numfmt-slm
05
⏳ Under Review IEEE SILCON 2026
Found but Not Used: A Full-Factorial Audit of RAG Distraction in Small Language Models
Tripathi, S., Kundu, S., Sur, S., and Singh, P. K.
Submitted to IEEE Silchar Subsection Conference (SILCON 2026)
▾
A full-factorial audit of retrieval-augmented generation across six small language models (360M–3.8B), isolating when retrieved context genuinely helps versus when it distracts. Examines the interaction of chunk size, retriever type, and top-k on downstream QA performance.
⌥ slm-rag-distraction-audit

Scaling Bias or Biasing Scale? Measuring Social Stereotype Bias Across Small Language Models
Sanbui, D., Bhol, S., Sur, S., and Singh, P. K.
Submitted to IEEE Silchar Subsection Conference (SILCON 2026)
▾
Measures social stereotype bias across ten small language models (135M–9B) on the BBQ, CrowS-Pairs, and StereoSet benchmarks — asking whether bias scales with model size or is an artefact of scale itself.
⌥ bias-scale-slm
07
⏳ Under Review ICCE 2026
Structured Communication and Ensemble Diversity in SLM-based Multi-Agent Systems: A Comparative Study
Sur, S., Sil Sarma, A., and Singh, P. K.
Submitted to International Conference for Convergence in Engineering (ICCE 2026)
▾
Two complementary multi-agent methodologies on the SVAMP benchmark across 5 SLMs (1.5B–7B): Pipeline Decomposition (Planner → Solver → Verifier with natural, constrained, and structured communication modes) and Diverse Ensemble Reasoning (parallel CoT, Formula-First, and Backward agents via majority vote). Best pipeline: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (67%); Diverse Ensemble achieved LLaMA 3.2-3B at 81% (vs. 77% CoT baseline). Introduced NATE (Normalized Accuracy per Thousand Tokens) as an efficiency metric.
⌥ SLM-based-MultiAgent-Frameworks
08
⏳ Under Review IEEE ICCCMLA 2026
Small Models, Big Choices: A Systematic Study of Few-Shot Prompting versus Fine-Tuning for Tiny Language Models
Dash, S., Pahari, D., Sur, S., and Singh, P. K.
Submitted to 2026 IEEE 8th International Conference on Cybernetics, Cognition and Machine Learning Applications (ICCCMLA)
▾
A systematic study of the few-shot vs. LoRA/QLoRA crossover threshold N* across 12 sub-1B language models and 5 NLP tasks — identifying the point at which fine-tuning begins to outperform in-context learning under realistic data budgets.
⌥ small-models-big-choices

Size Isn't Safety: Prompt-Injection Robustness of Small On-Device Language Models
Sur, S., Jariwala, K. B., Kundu, A., Mehta, P. D., and Singha Deb, R.
Submitted to International Conference on Machine Intelligence and Network Data Security (MINDS 2026)

⏳ Under Review MINDS 2026
Which Keypoints Matter? An Ablation Study for On-Device Indian Sign Language Recognition
Pahari, D., Sur, S., Rakshit, N., and Kundu, A.
Submitted to International Conference on Machine Intelligence and Network Data Security (MINDS 2026)
▾
Lightweight keypoint-based recognition of isolated Indian Sign Language on the INCLUDE-50 dataset, combining MediaPipe keypoints with small LSTM/Transformer heads. Achieves 97.8% top-1 accuracy at under 1 ms per clip on Apple Silicon, with an ablation isolating which keypoint groups actually carry the signal.
